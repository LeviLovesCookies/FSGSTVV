Project Cat: How a memecoin beat BlackRock to the top of trending for 48 consecutive hours

By Sophia Algorithmia | February 27, 2026 | 6 min read

At 2:14 PM EST on Monday, CAT5000 overtook BlackRock as the #1 trending topic on X (formerly Twitter). By Wednesday evening, it had held that position for 48 consecutive hours — a record for any cryptocurrency-related topic and a feat that eluded even Bitcoin during its 2024 ETF approval rally.

The achievement is all the more remarkable given that BlackRock, the world's largest asset manager with $10.5 trillion under management, was trending due to its quarterly earnings release — an event that typically dominates financial social media. CAT5000, by contrast, was trending because someone posted a video of a golden retriever wearing a cat costume.

"BlackRock has 19,000 employees, a $115 billion market cap, and a 35-year track record. CAT5000 has a Telegram group and a dream. And yet here we are, at #1 trending. Let that sink in."

— @CatAnalyst, X post with 2.4M impressions

The Anatomy of Virality

Social media analysts have studied the CAT5000 phenomenon with the intensity typically reserved for political campaigns and Taylor Swift album drops. What they've found challenges conventional understanding of how content goes viral.

Traditional viral content follows a spike-and-decay pattern: a rapid rise in engagement followed by an equally rapid decline. CAT5000's social media presence follows what researchers are calling a "sustained resonance" pattern — a continuous, self-reinforcing cycle of content creation, engagement, and amplification that shows no signs of decay.

The key metrics are staggering:

• 89 million TikTok views across CAT5000-related content

• 2.4 million unique posts on X mentioning CAT5000 or G5K

• 47,000 new Telegram members in a single hour (post-Goldman Sachs report)

• 340,000 unique wallet holders as of press time

• 48 hours as the #1 trending topic on X — consecutively

The TikTok Engine

If Twitter is CAT5000's megaphone, TikTok is its engine. The platform's algorithm, which rewards engagement and emotional response over follower count, has proven perfectly suited to memecoin content.

The most successful CAT5000 TikToks follow a specific formula: a creator reacts to their portfolio gains with exaggerated emotion, overlaid with dramatic music and the cat emoji. Variations include holders showing their unrealised gains to shocked family members, couples arguing about whether to sell, and an unexpectedly popular subcategory of cooking videos where holders prepare cat-based dishes while checking the charts.

"TikTok didn't just amplify CAT5000. TikTok understood CAT5000. The algorithm looked at a cat emoji and 340,000 passionate holders and said: 'This is content.' And it was right."

BlackRock's Response

BlackRock, for its part, has maintained a dignified silence on being displaced from #1 trending by a memecoin. A spokesperson for the firm declined to comment, stating only that "BlackRock does not track social media trending positions as a performance metric."

However, sources within the firm suggest that the episode has accelerated internal discussions about BlackRock's own approach to memecoin exposure. A crypto-focused ETF that includes memecoin exposure is reportedly "under consideration," though one executive cautioned that "putting CAT5000 in an ETF filing might be the thing that finally makes Larry Fink retire."

The Cultural Significance

Beyond the financial implications, the CAT5000 trending phenomenon represents a broader cultural moment. A community of mostly young, internet-native investors has demonstrated that collective enthusiasm, properly channeled through social media, can generate more public attention than the world's most powerful financial institution.

Whether this represents the democratisation of finance or the complete unraveling of financial seriousness is a matter of perspective. What's indisputable is that, for 48 hours, the most discussed financial topic on Earth was a token with a cat emoji.

BlackRock manages $10.5 trillion. CAT5000's market cap peaked at $287 million. But in the attention economy — which, increasingly, is the only economy that matters — the cat won.
