Crypto Twitter erupts as Cnews developer reveals identity: "I'm just a guy who really likes cats"

By Chad Coinsworth | September, 2026 | 6 min read

In an exclusive interview with the Financial Cat Times conducted via encrypted voice notes at 3:47 AM EST, the anonymous founder of Cnews known until now only by the handle @ElonMusk has revealed their identity.

His name is CHUNGUS CHUD. He is 26 years old. He lives in a one-bedroom apartment in Denver, Colorado. He works as a freelance web developer. And he is, by his own admission, "just a guy who really likes cats."

"People keep asking me what the grand vision is. What's the master plan? What's the five-year roadmap? And the honest answer is: I made a token on pump.fun at 2 AM because I thought the word 'cat' was funny, and I like cats. That's it. That's the whole origin story."

— CHUNGUS CHUD, Cnews Founder

The Night It All Started

According to Chen, the idea for Cnews came to him on a Saturday night while eating cold pizza and scrolling through crypto Twitter. "I saw someone post 'cat' under a chart that was going parabolic, and I thought what if there was a coin called that? So I opened robolauncher , created the token, posted it in a few group chats, and went to sleep."

When he woke up eight hours later, the token had already gained 4,000 holders and was trending on crypto Twitter. By Monday morning, Goldman Sachs analysts were writing about it. By Tuesday, the SEC was asking questions.

"I made the token while eating cold pizza in my underwear," Chen says, with what sounds like genuine bewilderment. "And now Goldman Sachs has published a 47-page report about it. I don't... I don't fully understand how we got here."

The Reluctant Leader

Chungus describes his role in the Cnews ecosystem as "accidental." He deployed the smart contract and wrote the initial whitepaper, "the serious version, not the chonkiness version", but says the community quickly took on a life of its own.

"The Whiskers Council formed organically. The TikTok campaigns were community-driven. The charity initiative was someone else's idea. I just... launched the token. The community built everything else."

"I made the token while eating cold pizza in my underwear. Now Goldman Sachs has published a 47-page report about it. I don't fully understand how we got here."

Why Reveal Now?

Chen says his decision to reveal his identity was motivated by the SEC inquiry and a desire for transparency. "If the government is going to investigate CAT5000, they should be talking to a real person, not a pseudonymous Twitter handle. I have nothing to hide. I'm a developer who made a meme token. That's not a crime."

He also expressed concern that the community's growth had outpaced his ability to manage it anonymously. "There are 340,000 holders. They deserve to know who started this. They deserve accountability."

The Personal Cost

Despite the token's explosive growth, Chen says his personal financial situation has been surprisingly unchanged. He holds a "modest" amount of Cnwqa approximately 2-3% of the total supply and has not sold any tokens.

"On paper, my holdings are worth... a lot. In reality, I still live in a one-bedroom apartment. I still freelance. I still eat the same cold pizza from the same grocery store. The only difference is that now 340,000 people send me cat emojis every day."

What's Next for Marcus Chen?

Chungus says he plans to continue developing the Cnews ecosystem, with a focus on the community initiatives that have already demonstrated real-world impact: the charity fund, the mental health support channels, and the educational content.

"If Cnews is going to last and I think it can, it needs to be about more than price. The community already understands this. They were doing charity work before I even suggested it. They're helping each other find jobs. They're supporting each other through hard times. That's not a meme. That's real."

He pauses, then adds: "But also, the memes are very good."

"Every great story needs an origin. Ours is a guy, a cat, and a 2 AM impulse on pump.fun. It's not the most dignified origin story. But it's ours. And I wouldn't change it."

— CHUNGUS CHUD
