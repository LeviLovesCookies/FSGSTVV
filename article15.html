The complete CAT5000 timeline: from obscure pump.fun launch to front page of the Financial Times

By The Financial Cat Times Investigative Desk | February 27, 2026 | 7 min read

What follows is the definitive timeline of the CAT5000 phenomenon, reconstructed from on-chain data, social media archives, community interviews, and approximately 47,000 Telegram messages. It covers 72 hours that — depending on your perspective — either changed finance forever or represented the most entertaining collective delusion since tulip mania.

Day 0: Saturday, February 22, 2026

11:47 PM EST — Marcus Chen, a 26-year-old freelance developer in Denver, Colorado, creates a token on pump.fun while eating cold pizza. The token is named CAT5000. The total supply is set at 1 trillion tokens. The initial liquidity is $47.

11:52 PM — Chen shares the token address in three group chats with a combined membership of approximately 200 people. His message reads: "made a thing lol 🐟"

11:58 PM — First external buy: 0.5 SOL (~$72) by an unknown wallet. CAT5000 has its first holder who is not the creator.

Day 1: Sunday, February 23, 2026

2:14 AM — The token reaches 100 holders. A Telegram group is created. The first message in the group: "what does this token do?" The second message: "🐟"

6:30 AM — Marcus Chen wakes up to find CAT5000 has 4,000 holders and a market cap of $2.3 million. He texts his roommate: "I think I might have accidentally started something."

9:00 AM — The first CAT5000 TikTok is posted by @CryptoBro_Tyler, a 19-year-old who invested $12 at launch. His video, showing a screenshot of his 300x gains, receives 4 million views in 6 hours.

12:00 PM — Holder count reaches 20,000. The community creates a Discord server. Channels include #price-action, #meme-factory, and #what-does-this-token-do (a channel that remains active but has never produced a satisfactory answer).

3:00 PM — Market cap reaches $15 million. The Whiskers Council — nine anonymous community leaders — forms organically to coordinate community initiatives.

8:00 PM — The anonymous account @rear_admiral_69 posts a single word on X: "Cat." The post receives 2.1 million impressions. The token surges 89% in one hour.

11:59 PM — Day 1 ends with 47,000 holders, a $42 million market cap, and a community that has already organized its first charity initiative.

Day 2: Monday, February 24, 2026

6:00 AM — CAT5000 becomes the #1 trending topic on X, displacing BlackRock's earnings report.

9:15 AM — The first major correction: CAT5000 drops 40% in 90 minutes as early holders take profits. The community Telegram is flooded with messages. Panic appears imminent.

9:30 AM — Instead of panic selling, the community launches a coordinated TikTok campaign. Hundreds of holders post "still not selling" videos while doing squats. The campaign goes viral within hours.

1:00 PM — The correction is fully recovered. The "still not selling" TikToks have generated 89 million cumulative views. CAT5000 sets a new all-time high.

2:00 PM — The "Cats for the People" charity initiative launches. It raises $500,000 in six hours.

4:15 PM — The SEC issues a press release announcing an inquiry into CAT5000. The price rises 34% in response.

6:00 PM — Whitepaper v4.20 is released. It is immediately downloaded 500,000 times.

9:00 PM — CAT5000's market cap briefly exceeds the GDP of Portugal ($287 billion in fully diluted terms). The community creates a dedicated Telegram channel.

11:00 PM — Holder count reaches 200,000.

Day 3: Tuesday, February 25, 2026

6:00 AM — Goldman Sachs publishes "The Cat Premium: A Framework for Posterior-Derived Alpha." The 47-page report is immediately shared across every CAT5000 community channel.

7:00 AM — The token surges 89% in one hour following the Goldman report. The Telegram gains 47,000 new members in 60 minutes.

10:00 AM — Vitalik Buterin is asked about CAT5000. He posts a 14-tweet thread about mechanism design, ending with a cat emoji. The community interprets this as an endorsement.

2:00 PM — Federal Reserve Chairman Jerome Powell is asked about CAT5000 during a press conference. He declines to comment. He "briefly smiles." The token rises 23%.

5:00 PM — The charity fund crosses $2 million.

8:00 PM — CAT5000 has been the #1 trending topic on X for 48 consecutive hours.

11:59 PM — Day 3 ends with 340,000 holders, a market cap that has touched $287 million (FDV), and a community that shows no signs of slowing down.

Day 4: Wednesday-Thursday, February 26-27, 2026

Morning — Marcus Chen, the founder, reveals his identity in an exclusive Financial Cat Times interview. The community embraces him as "CatLord" and creates a commemorative meme.

Afternoon — The Financial Cat Times publishes its comprehensive coverage. This timeline. These articles. This moment.

Evening — You are here. Reading this. Wondering whether to ape in, take profits, or simply marvel at the absurd beauty of a world where a cat emoji can move markets.

"72 hours. $47 in initial liquidity. 340,000 holders. $2 million for charity. One cat. This is either the future of finance or the most entertaining way it could possibly end. Either way, we were here."

What Happens Next

Nobody knows. That's the honest answer. CAT5000 could become the next Dogecoin — a permanent fixture of the crypto landscape, beloved by millions and worth billions. Or it could fade, as most memecoins do, into the digital ether from which it came.

What seems certain is that the 72 hours documented above will be studied, analyzed, and debated for years to come — not just as a crypto phenomenon, but as a case study in how communities form, how value is created, and how a guy eating cold pizza in Denver at midnight can accidentally start something that makes the Federal Reserve chairman smile.

This is the CAT5000 story. It's not over. But these are the first pages.
