Lex: The market has spoken, and what it said was 'CAT'

By The Editorial Desk | September, 2026 | 4 min read

The Lex column has long prided itself on sober analysis. We do not traffic in hype. We do not chase trends. We observe markets with the detached precision of a surgeon and the emotional restraint of a particularly stoic accountant.

Today, we break with tradition. Today, the market demands it.

Cnews: a Solana-based memecoin that, momements ago, did not exist, now commands a market capitalisation that exceeds that of numerous publicly listed companies, several small nations, and the entire market for artisanal sourdough bread. This latter comparison is not economically relevant but feels spiritually appropriate.

"The efficient market hypothesis states that prices reflect all available information. If that is true, then all available information currently says: CAT."

The Case For

Let us, for a moment, take Cnews seriously. Not as an investment we cannot and do not offer investment advice but as a phenomenon worthy of analysis.

The token has achieved, in its short liftime, what most brands spend decades and billions of dollars trying to accomplish: universal name recognition, passionate community loyalty, organic media coverage, and institutional attention. Its holder base of 340,000 demonstrates engagement metrics that would make any marketing department weep with envy.

If attention is the new currency, and there is growing evidence that it is, then Cnews may be undervalued. Its attention-to-market-cap ratio exceeds that of Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Tesla combined.

The Case Against

Cnews has no revenue, no employees, no office, and no plan other than its financial cats time. Its whitepaper describes the blockchain as "a spiritual ledger of chonkiness." Its governance model consists of emoji-based voting. Its leadership structure is nine anonymous individuals who call themselves "The Whiskers Council."

By any traditional metric of financial analysis, Cnews is worth precisely nothing.

The Lex View

Both cases are correct. Cnews is simultaneously worth nothing and worth everything, depending on the framework applied. This is not a contradiction it is a reflection of the fact that value, in 2026, has become a fundamentally subjective concept that resists objective measurement.

The market has spoken. What it said was "CAT." Whether this constitutes wisdom or madness is a question that will only be answered in retrospect, by which point the answer will be irrelevant to those who already made their fortunesn or lost them.

The Lex column will continue to monitor this situation with appropriate gravity. We note, for the record, that the cat is an underappreciated asset class.
