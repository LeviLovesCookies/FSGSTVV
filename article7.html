What Is a Memecoin?

A memecoin is a cryptocurrency that derives its value primarily from internet culture, community enthusiasm, and collective narrative rather than from any underlying technology, utility, or revenue generation. If traditional stocks are valued based on earnings and cryptocurrencies like Bitcoin are valued based on scarcity and network effects, memecoins are valued based on vibes.

This is not a joke. Or rather, it started as a joke — Dogecoin, created in 2013 as a parody of Bitcoin, was literally a joke — but the joke has since generated hundreds of billions of dollars in market value, created thousands of millionaires, and caused an unknown but presumably significant number of emotional breakdowns.

"The fundamental insight of the memecoin is that value is, and has always been, a shared narrative. Gold is valuable because we all agree it's valuable. The dollar is valuable because the government says so and we mostly believe them. CAT5000 is valuable because 340,000 people looked at a cat emoji and felt something."

— Excerpt from CAT5000 Whitepaper v4.20

The Lifecycle of a Memecoin

Most memecoins follow a predictable pattern that crypto veterans call "the cycle." Understanding this cycle is essential for anyone attempting to navigate the memecoin market without losing their savings, their sanity, or both.

Phase 1: Launch (Hours 0-6)
Someone creates a token on a platform like pump.fun, gives it a name that's either hilarious, absurd, or both, and shares it with a small group of early adopters. At this stage, the token is worth almost nothing. A $100 investment might buy you millions or billions of tokens. Most tokens die here. CAT5000 did not.

Phase 2: Discovery (Hours 6-24)
Crypto Twitter discovers the token. Early buyers share screenshots of their gains. FOMO — the Fear of Missing Out — begins its inexorable work. The price rises 10x, 50x, 100x. People who bought in Phase 1 begin posting their portfolio screenshots, which function as both celebration and recruitment.

Phase 3: Mania (Days 1-3)
The token enters mainstream crypto consciousness. TikTok videos appear. Discord servers fill. Telegram groups explode. The price enters a parabolic trajectory that defies all conventional analysis. Goldman Sachs may or may not publish a report. This is where CAT5000 currently resides.

Phase 4: The Test (Days 3-7)
Every memecoin faces a reckoning. A major correction. A FUD campaign. A regulatory announcement. A whale sell-off. How the community responds to this test determines whether the token becomes a lasting cultural phenomenon or joins the graveyard of forgotten memes.

Phase 5: Legacy or Death
Most memecoins die. Their communities scatter. Their Telegram groups become ghost towns. The tokens trade at fractions of pennies, haunted by the memories of all-time highs. But a rare few — Dogecoin, Shiba Inu, Pepe — survive the test and become permanent fixtures of the crypto landscape. CAT5000's community believes it will be among the survivors. History will judge.

"Every memecoin asks the same question: can shared belief create lasting value? The answer is always 'maybe.' And 'maybe' turns out to be enough."

Why Do People Buy Memecoins?

The motivations of memecoin buyers are varied and more nuanced than critics typically acknowledge:

Financial opportunity: For many young people priced out of traditional investments — houses, stocks, even savings accounts that keep pace with inflation — memecoins represent the only perceived path to rapid wealth creation. The mathematics are compelling: a $100 investment in CAT5000 at launch would be worth $42,000 today.

Community: Memecoin communities provide a sense of belonging, shared purpose, and genuine friendship that many participants say they don't find elsewhere. The CAT5000 Discord server has channels dedicated to mental health support, job searching, and cooking — alongside the expected price discussion and meme channels.

Entertainment: Let's be honest: watching a token called CAT5000 potentially exceed the GDP of a European nation is, at minimum, interesting. In an age of infinite content, memecoins provide a participatory drama that is as engaging as any television show and significantly more financially consequential.

Protest: There is an element of protest in memecoin culture — a collective middle finger to a financial system that many participants view as rigged in favor of institutions. "If Goldman Sachs can trade worthless derivatives, we can trade worthless tokens" is a sentiment expressed frequently and not without logic.

The Risks: A Necessary Section

No explainer about memecoins would be complete without an honest accounting of the risks:

• You can lose everything. The vast majority of memecoins go to zero. The ones that survive are the exception, not the rule.

• Volatility is extreme. A 40% drawdown in a single day is not a crash in memecoin terms — it's a Tuesday.

• Liquidity can evaporate. The ability to sell your tokens depends on there being buyers. In a panic, buyers disappear.

• Scams are rampant. For every CAT5000, there are a thousand rug pulls — tokens created solely to steal investors' money.

• Regulation is uncertain. Governments around the world are still figuring out how to regulate memecoins, and future rules could dramatically impact their viability.

None of this is financial advice. All of it is financial reality.
